How this works. Geometry (aperture radius, sag, edge angle, geometric focus) is exact for any input. At the standard FUS Instruments transducer's own 35 mm/24.5 mm geometry and 0.5 or 1.45 MHz, every number here is read directly from a 1 mm-step k-Wave simulation sweep (✓ simulated) — including the minimum-height focal collapse, a real effect where a too-short cone leaves an opening as wide as the aperture itself, creating a near-flat resin shoulder parallel to the transducer face and destroying the coherent focus even though raw power throughput stays fine.
  • Away from those exact points, the same shape of relationship is ≈ estimated by interpolating between the two simulated frequencies (diffraction-limited spot size and the collapse-height threshold both scale with wavelength) — reasonable near 0.5–1.45 MHz, increasingly speculative outside it.
  • Changing diameter/ROC recomputes exact geometry, but the diffraction and collapse-height models keep the same calibration from the original transducer — a genuinely different bowl should be validated with its own simulation before building.
  • All of this assumes a water-equivalent target medium and a 3 mm resin wall, matching the rest of the project.
